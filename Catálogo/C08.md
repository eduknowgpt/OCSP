# Ciclo de Vida da Competência C08 — *Identify Turing Machine Variants*


## 1. Definição Original — *CSP-Report (Ciclo 1)*

### Emergência da Competência

A competência **C08 – Identify Turing Machine Variants** emerge a partir da análise da tarefa *Traffic Control*, na qual os estudantes são expostos à necessidade de **avaliar a adequação do modelo computacional** frente a requisitos específicos, como contagem eficiente, manipulação de dados e complexidade operacional.

Embora a tarefa possa ser resolvida com uma **Turing Machine padrão**, o contexto instrucional prevê que os estudantes **reconheçam a existência de variantes** (e.g., multi-tape, não determinísticas) e compreendam suas **diferenças conceituais e implicações computacionais**. Essa análise é necessária para justificar decisões de modelagem, ainda que a implementação final não utilize tais extensões.

### Formulação Inicial da Competência

Na fase de *Competency Authoring*, a C08 foi especificada como uma competência de natureza **conceitual e analítica**, com foco em:

* identificar diferentes variantes de Turing Machines;
* compreender suas características fundamentais;
* reconhecer limitações e capacidades relativas.


### Primeira Especificação (CSP Phase 1B: Competency Authoring)

### Competency C08 Specification

### Competency Title

    Identify Turing Machine Variants


### Textual Description

Understand and identify different **variants of Turing Machines**—such as multi-tape, non-deterministic, or other extensions—by analyzing their computational capabilities and constraints. Students are expected to evaluate whether the use of standard Turing Machines is sufficient or if extended models are necessary to effectively address system requirements.

This competency reflects a conceptual understanding of the **Church-Turing Thesis**, and requires students to apply **Analytical and Critical Thinking (FPK)** to assess system needs and propose appropriate modeling approaches based on the specific characteristics of Turing Machine variants.


### Knowledge Specification

The following knowledge areas are critical for this competency:

* **Turing Machine Variants**
  * Involves recognizing different types of Turing Machines (e.g., multi-tape, non-deterministic) and understanding their operational implications for modeling and problem-solving.

* **Church-Turing Thesis**
  * Provides a theoretical foundation to assess the expressive power and feasibility of formal models used in real-world algorithmic problems.

* **Analytical and Critical Thinking (FPK)**
  * Supports the evaluation of system requirements and the selection of the most appropriate computational models to meet functional objectives.



### Disposition Specification

As the *Traffic Control* task is carried out collaboratively, and involves analysis and decision-making under complex conditions, the following behavioral dispositions are essential:

* **Inventive**
  Demonstrate curiosity and rigor in exploring the computational implications of various Turing Machine extensions.

* **Collaborative**
  Engage in collective analysis and justification of modeling decisions, contributing to team-based evaluation of alternatives.

* **Responsible**
  Ensure that modeling decisions are logically consistent, grounded in theory, and meet the problem’s specifications.

* **Proactive**
  Take initiative in exploring and proposing more expressive computational solutions where needed.



### Knowledge-Skill Pairing

This step maps **knowledge areas to the corresponding skills** required to successfully demonstrate competency in this task.

#### Mapping Knowledge to Skills
To demonstrate this competency, students must show the ability to:

* **Apply** Analytical and Critical Thinking to evaluate system requirements and determine whether standard or variant Turing Machines should be used.

* **Understand** Turing Machine Variants to distinguish between their capabilities and select the model best suited to the problem’s constraints.

* **Understand** the Church-Turing Thesis to justify, at a conceptual level, the feasibility and completeness of using extended Turing Machine models.



#### Bloom’s Taxonomy Alignment

* *Apply* level is used to assess the ability to analyze the problem, evaluate modeling options, and justify the selection of computational variants.

* *Understand* level is used to evaluate conceptual knowledge of both the **Turing Machine variants** and the **Church-Turing Thesis**, ensuring students can explain and justify their modeling decisions.



#### Verb Annotation
To provide clarity on competency expectations, the following verb annotations define the required actions:

* **Apply** → Analytical and Critical Thinking → *Evaluate, Decide, Justify*
* **Understand** → Turing Machine  → *Differentiate, Recognize, Explain*
* **Understand** → Church-Turing Thesis → *Interpret, Justify, Reflect*

## 2. Revisões — *CSRP-Report (Ciclo 1 — Revisão por Especialistas)*

### Principais Achados do CSRP

A revisão por especialistas confirmou a **pertinência instrucional da C08**, mas identificou pontos críticos de ajuste:

* A inclusão da **Church–Turing Thesis** foi considerada **teoricamente válida**, porém **não essencial** para a demonstração da competência no contexto da tarefa *Traffic Control*;
* Parte da descrição permanecia **excessivamente abstrata**, sem conexão clara com decisões observáveis dentro da atividade;
* O **verbo nuclear (*Identify*) e o nível cognitivo (*Understand*)** foram considerados **adequados e bem alinhados** à intenção formativa da competência.

Os especialistas enfatizaram que a competência deveria apoiar a **capacidade de reconhecimento e diferenciação de modelos**, e não a discussão metateórica sobre computabilidade.

### Decisão Técnica do CSRP

* **Refinar os conhecimentos associados**, removendo elementos não operacionalizados pela tarefa;
* Reforçar o vínculo entre a identificação de variantes e a **avaliação da adequação do modelo** frente a requisitos do problema.



## 3. Alterações Implementadas — *CSP-Adjustments*

### Ajustes Aplicados

Com base nas recomendações do CSRP, a competência C08 passou pelos seguintes refinamentos:

* **Remoção da Church–Turing Thesis** do conjunto de conhecimentos associados, restringindo a competência ao que é efetivamente mobilizado na tarefa;

* Consolidação do conhecimento em torno de **Turing Machines** e **Turing Machine Variants**, com maior precisão terminológica;

* Revisão textual da descrição da competência para enfatizar:

  * a **identificação** de variantes;
  * a **diferenciação conceitual** entre modelos;
  * a **avaliação de adequação** frente a requisitos do problema.

### Estado Final da Competência

### Competency C08 Specification

### Competency Title

    Identify Turing Machine Variants


### Competency Description

Demonstrate conceptual understanding of Turing Machine variants by identifying, differentiating, and comparing foundational and extended models—such as multi-tape and non-deterministic Turing Machines—with respect to their computational characteristics, limitations, and applicability.

This competency focuses on the ability to analyze formal model definitions and evaluate the adequacy of standard versus extended Turing Machine models in relation to specific problem requirements. Learners must apply analytical and critical thinking to justify modeling choices and explain how different variants influence the expressiveness and efficiency of problem-solving strategies, without engaging in implementation or metatheoretical discussion.



### **Knowledge Specification**

The following knowledge areas are essential for demonstrating this competency:

* **Turing Machine Variants**
  Knowledge of the main **extensions of the standard Turing Machine model**—such as **multi-tape** and **non-deterministic** machines—and understanding of their **conceptual characteristics, limitations, and comparative implications** for computational modeling.

* **Analytical and Critical Thinking (FPK)**
  Ability to **analyze problem requirements**, compare alternative computational models, and **reason about the adequacy** of different Turing Machine variants with respect to expressiveness and efficiency, without engaging in implementation.



### **Disposition Specification**

The *Traffic Control* task requires behavioral dispositions that support careful analysis and justified decision-making in collaborative contexts:

* **Inventive** — explore alternative formal models and reason about their applicability;
* **Collaborative** — engage constructively in collective analysis and justification of modeling choices;
* **Responsible** — ensure that comparisons and conclusions are logically sound and grounded in formal definitions;
* **Proactive** — take initiative in examining and discussing modeling alternatives when standard approaches may be insufficient.



Segue a **versão aprimorada da seção de *Knowledge–Skill Pairing***, **totalmente alinhada às revisões do CSRP**, com **remoção explícita da Church–Turing Thesis**, **clareza cognitiva** e **separação nítida entre compreender e aplicar**.



### **Knowledge–Skill Pairing**

This section maps the **relevant knowledge areas** to the **observable skills** required to demonstrate this competency in a consistent and assessable manner.

### **Mapping Knowledge to Skills**

To demonstrate this competency, learners must be able to:

* **Apply** **Analytical and Critical Thinking (FPK)** to **analyze system requirements**, compare alternative computational models, and determine whether a **standard Turing Machine** or a **variant** (e.g., multi-tape or non-deterministic) is conceptually more appropriate for a given problem.

* **Understand** **Turing Machine Variants** by **recognizing, differentiating, and explaining** their defining characteristics, limitations, and relative advantages, without engaging in implementation or simulation.



### **Bloom’s Taxonomy Alignment**

* **Apply**
  Used to assess the learner’s ability to **analyze problem requirements**, **evaluate modeling alternatives**, and **justify conceptual decisions** regarding the adequacy of different Turing Machine models.

* **Understand**
  Used to assess **conceptual knowledge of Turing Machine variants**, ensuring that learners can **explain differences**, **recognize appropriate contexts**, and **articulate reasoning** behind model selection.



## **Verb Annotation**

The following verb annotations clarify the expected performance:

* **Apply** → *Analytical and Critical Thinking (FPK)*
  **Verbs:** *Evaluate, Decide, Justify*

* **Understand** → *Turing Machine Variants*
  **Verbs:** *Differentiate, Recognize, Explain*
