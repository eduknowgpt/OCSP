## Description

This competency encompasses the ability to **analyze problem requirements and develop computational solutions grounded in finite automata as formal models of computation**. It requires a solid understanding of **automata theory**, enabling learners to transform informal or semi-formal problem descriptions into **precise automaton specifications** that are logically sound and operationally viable.

Learners demonstrate this competency by **modeling system behavior through states, transitions, and acceptance conditions**, ensuring that the constructed automata accurately capture the intended functional requirements. This includes reasoning about **input handling, state progression, and boundary conditions**, as well as making design decisions that promote correctness, clarity, and efficiency within the expressive limits of finite automata.

Additionally, learners are expected to **adapt and extend automaton models** to accommodate new constraints or problem variations—such as additional input symbols, modified acceptance criteria, or enhanced behavioral rules—demonstrating **flexibility, abstraction skills, and systematic problem-solving** in the context of formal computational models.
