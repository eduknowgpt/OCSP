
# Ciclo de Vida da Competência C09 — *Make Use of Turing Machine Variants*


## 1. Definição Original — *CSP-Report (Ciclo 1)*

### Emergência da Competência

A competência **C09** emerge da própria natureza da tarefa *Traffic Control*, que envolve **processamento e manipulação estruturada de dados** (sensores de entrada/saída, classificação por limiares, contagem e comparação de frequências). Esse tipo de solução pode demandar **estratégias de modelagem** mais eficientes do que uma Turing Machine padrão, especialmente quando a representação do problema exige múltiplas trilhas de computação, organização de dados intermediários ou redução de complexidade operacional.

Assim, além de reconhecer variantes (C08), o processo de autoria identificou a necessidade de uma competência distinta voltada ao **uso aplicado de variantes** como recurso de modelagem, com foco em **adaptação do modelo formal às exigências do problema**.

### Formulação Inicial da Competência

Na fase de *Competency Authoring*, a C09 foi especificada como competência de natureza **operacional e aplicada**, centrada em:

* selecionar uma variante adequada (p.ex., multi-tape, não determinística),
* adaptar a solução ao modelo escolhido,
* justificar a escolha em função de requisitos e restrições.


### Primeira Especificação (CSP Phase 1B: Competency Authoring)

### Competency C09 Specification

### Competency Title

  Make Use of Turing Machine Variants


### Textual Description

Understand and apply different **variants of Turing Machines**—such as multi-tape and non-deterministic models—to develop optimized solutions for computational problems.This competency involves analyzing system requirements and determining whether standard Turing Machines are sufficient or if extended capabilities are needed.

Students are expected to demonstrate a conceptual understanding of the **Church-Turing Thesis**, associating the formal model of computation with the intuitive notion of an algorithm. They must also apply analytical reasoning to identify the most suitable computational model for the problem at hand.


### Knowledge Specification

The following knowledge areas are critical for this competency:

* **Turing Machine Variants**
  Understanding alternative computational models (e.g., multi-tape, non-deterministic) and their expressive and operational differences compared to standard Turing Machines.

* **Church-Turing Thesis**
  Grasping the theoretical basis that connects formal models of computation with algorithmic reasoning, and using this to justify the feasibility and completeness of proposed solutions.

* **Analytical and Critical Thinking (FPK)**
  Essential for evaluating problem requirements and selecting the most appropriate modeling approach based on complexity, efficiency, and expressiveness.



### Disposition Specification

The analysis of the *Traffic Control on Aratu Road* task highlights behavioral attributes that support the critical evaluation and adaptation of computational models to suit practical needs. The main dispositions include:

* **Investigative**
  Explore alternative computational models and assess their relevance to the problem context.

* **Responsible**
  Justify modeling decisions with theoretical and practical consistency, ensuring that chosen solutions meet the required objectives.

* **Proactive**
  Anticipate potential limitations in standard models and propose improved alternatives through variants.

* **Collaborative**
  Discuss and validate modeling choices with peers to enhance the robustness of the solution.

* **Creative**
  Combine formal knowledge and contextual insight to construct alternative models that are efficient and innovative.



### Knowledge-Skill Pairing

This step maps **knowledge areas to the corresponding skills** required to successfully demonstrate competency in this task.

#### Mapping Knowledge to Skills
To demonstrate this competency, students must show the ability to:

* **Apply** Analytical and Critical Thinking to examine system requirements and determine whether standard or variant Turing Machines are more appropriate.

* **Use** Turing Machine Variants to model and optimize computational solutions in scenarios where standard machines are insufficient.

* **Understand** the Church-Turing Thesis to conceptually justify the use of formal computation models in real-world problem-solving.



#### Bloom’s Taxonomy Alignment

* *Apply* level is used to assess the student's ability to analyze requirements and choose the appropriate Turing Machine model accordingly.

* *Understand* level is used to evaluate the student’s comprehension of the Church-Turing Thesis and their ability to justify solution feasibility based on it.



#### Verb Annotation
To provide clarity on competency expectations, the following verb annotations define the required actions:

* **Apply** → Analytical and Critical Thinking → *Evaluate, Decide, Select*
* **Apply** → Turing Machine  → *Use, Adapt, Implement*
* **Understand** → Church-Turing Thesis → *Explain, Justify, Interpret*

## 2. Revisões — *CSRP-Report (Ciclo 1 — Revisão por Especialistas)*

### Principais achados do CSRP

A revisão por especialistas reforçou que a C09 é pertinente, mas exigia controle de escopo:

* **Church–Turing Thesis** foi considerada **não essencial** para o desempenho da competência no contexto da tarefa, pois não é mobilizada como requisito operacional para aplicar variantes na modelagem;
* necessidade de **maior ancoragem em comportamentos observáveis**, evitando descrições genéricas ou excessivamente narrativas;
* recomendação de **refinar granularidade do conhecimento** para que a competência reflita diretamente o que os estudantes fazem ao empregar variantes (p.ex., escolher multi-tape para separar trilhas de dados, ou justificar não determinismo em certas decomposições).

### Decisão técnica do CSRP

* **Manter C09**, preservando seu papel aplicado e distinto da C08;
* **Remover conteúdo teórico não operacionalizado** (Church–Turing Thesis) dos conhecimentos associados;
* **Fortalecer a descrição** para explicitar o que significa “usar variantes” no desempenho: seleção justificada, adaptação do modelo e validação/adequação ao problema.




## 3. Alterações Implementadas — *CSP-Adjustments*

### Ajustes aplicados

Com base nas recomendações do CSRP, foram implementados refinamentos que estabilizam a C09:

* **Remoção da Church–Turing Thesis** como conhecimento associado (e, quando aplicável, também dos objetivos específicos relacionados);
* consolidação do foco em:

  * **Turing Machine Variants** (multi-tape, não determinística, outras extensões relevantes ao curso),
  * **Analytical and Critical Thinking (FPK)** como suporte explícito à seleção e adaptação;
* reescrita da competência para enfatizar:

  * **seleção orientada por requisitos**,
  * **aplicação/adaptação do modelo**,
  * **justificativa técnica** da escolha,
  * distinção clara de C08 (*Identify*) e C07 (*Develop*).


### Estado Final da Competência

### Competency C09 Specification

### **Competency Title**

    Apply Turing Machine Variants

### **Competency Description**

Demonstrate the ability to select and apply appropriate variants of Turing Machines—such as multi-tape or non-deterministic models—to design computational solutions that are adequately tailored to specific problem requirements.

This competency involves analyzing the demands of a computational task, determining whether a standard Turing Machine is sufficient, or whether the use of a variant provides advantages in efficiency, organization, or clarity of modeling. Learners must base their decisions on explicit problem constraints and justify the selection of a particular variant accordingly.

Through the application of analytical reasoning and formal modeling skills, students are expected to adapt and use Turing Machine variants to represent and solve formal problems, demonstrating that the chosen model effectively supports the intended computational behavior without engaging in theoretical equivalence or metatheoretical discussion.



## **Knowledge Specification**

The following knowledge areas are essential for demonstrating this competency:

* **Turing Machine Variants**
  - Knowledge of **extended Turing Machine models**—such as **multi-tape** and **non-deterministic machines**—and understanding of their **operational characteristics, expressive capabilities, and practical implications** when compared to the standard Turing Machine model.

* **Analytical and Critical Thinking (FPK)**
  - Ability to **evaluate problem requirements**, reason about modeling alternatives, and **select an appropriate Turing Machine variant** based on criteria such as efficiency, clarity of representation, and adequacy to task constraints.



## **Disposition Specification**

The *Traffic Control* task requires behavioral dispositions that support the **effective application and adaptation** of computational models in a collaborative and problem-oriented context:

* **Investigative** — explore alternative Turing Machine variants and assess their suitability for the problem at hand;
* **Responsible** — justify modeling decisions with logical consistency and adherence to task requirements;
* **Proactive** — anticipate limitations of standard models and propose improved solutions using variants;
* **Collaborative** — discuss, validate, and refine modeling choices with peers;
* **Creative** — combine formal knowledge and contextual insight to construct efficient and well-adapted computational models.



## **Knowledge–Skill Pairing**

This section maps the **relevant knowledge areas** to the **observable skills** required to successfully demonstrate this competency in an applied and assessable manner.

### **Mapping Knowledge to Skills**

To demonstrate this competency, learners must be able to:

* **Apply** **Analytical and Critical Thinking (FPK)** to **analyze system requirements**, evaluate modeling alternatives, and determine whether a **standard Turing Machine** or a **variant** is more appropriate for the problem context.

* **Apply** **Turing Machine Variants** to **model, adapt, and optimize computational solutions** in situations where the standard model is insufficient or suboptimal, ensuring that the selected variant adequately supports the intended computational behavior.



## **Bloom’s Taxonomy Alignment**

* **Apply**
  Used to assess the learner’s ability to **analyze requirements**, **select an appropriate Turing Machine variant**, and **operationalize that choice** through concrete modeling decisions.

This alignment reflects the applied nature of the competency, distinguishing it from:

* **C08** (*Identify Turing Machine Variants* — conceptual recognition), and
* **C07** (*Develop Problem-Solving Solutions Using Turing Machines* — full solution construction).



## **Verb Annotation**

The following verb annotations clarify the expected performance:

* **Apply** → *Analytical and Critical Thinking (FPK)*
  **Verbs:** *Evaluate, Decide, Select*

* **Apply** → *Turing Machine Variants*
  **Verbs:** *Use, Adapt, Implement*
