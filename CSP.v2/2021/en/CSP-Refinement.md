## CSP Refinement Report

During the implementation of the Competency Specification Process (CSP), a number of insights and enhancements emerged organically from the practical application of the method. These improvements were driven by reflections from the authors during Phase 1 and refined through collaborative input from expert reviewers in Phase 2.

One of the main challenges identified early in Phase 1 was the selection of the most appropriate cognitive level in Bloom’s revised taxonomy for each knowledge–skill pairing. To make these choices more transparent and pedagogically justifiable, the authors adopted a practice of explicitly selecting taxonomy verbs that clarified the intended cognitive complexity.

This practice was well received by reviewers in Phase 2, who recommended that, beyond selecting the verbs, additional annotations should be included to further justify and document the rationale for each choice. This feedback led to the creation of the "Bloom’s Taxonomy Alignment" section within the competency specification template. This new subsection documents the cognitive intentions behind each pairing, as interpreted by both the competency authors and reviewers, thereby enhancing the transparency and pedagogical rigor of the specification.

Additionally, given that the initial implementation of the CSP followed a relatively ad hoc structure, there emerged a clear need to formalize and standardize the process. As a result, a suite of structured reporting templates was developed to guide each phase and ensure procedural consistency across different contexts. These include:

* **Instructional Entity Analysis Report**
* **CSP Phase 1A & 1B Template Report**
* **CSP Phase 2 – Expert Review Template Report**
* **CSP Review Protocol**
* **CSP Phase 3 – Semantic Structuring Report**

These templates evolved in parallel with the CSP itself, being progressively refined as competencies were developed and validated across multiple tasks and review cycles. Each template not only supports internal consistency and methodological rigor but also promotes reuse and adaptability of the process in varied instructional contexts. Together, these refinements have contributed to transforming the CSP into a robust, transparent, and replicable framework for competency modeling in education.
