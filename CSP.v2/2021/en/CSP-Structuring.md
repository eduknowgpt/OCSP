## Competency Specialization through Contextual Scope Expansion (C07–C09)

During the progressive application of the **Competency Specification Process (CSP)** across Tasks 02 to 05, it became evident that certain competencies—specifically **C07 (Develop solutions using Turing Machines)**, **C08 (Identify Turing Machine variants)**, and **C09 (Apply Turing Machine variants)**—were being **reused in contexts that significantly exceeded their original instructional scope**.

Rather than indicating flaws in the original specifications, this evolution reflects a **natural pedagogical progression**: competencies initially defined for **single-agent, localized modeling tasks** were later enacted within **integrated, multi-agent, and system-level problem scenarios**. This shift required a formal response at the competency-model level.

To preserve **conceptual clarity, assessability, and CSP transparency**, the decision was made to **specialize**, rather than merely reuse or overload, the affected competencies.



## From Reuse to Explicit Specialization

In Tasks 02–04, competencies C07–C09 were applied in contexts characterized by:

* isolated computational models;
* local problem constraints;
* limited interaction between components.

By contrast, **Task 05 – *The Farmer Robot and the Feeder Robot*** introduces:

* **multiple autonomous agents**;
* **shared or coordinated computational behavior**;
* **system-level requirements and constraints**;
* reliance on a **unified computational formalism (Turing Machines)**.

This change alters **what it means to demonstrate the same nominal competency**. For example:

* *Developing* a Turing Machine in isolation (C07) is not equivalent to
  *developing and coordinating multiple Turing Machines* across agents.
* *Identifying* variants conceptually (C08) differs from
  *analyzing their adequacy in integrated systems*.
* *Applying* variants locally (C09) is distinct from
  *coordinating and justifying their use at the system level*.

Treating these expanded enactments as simple reuse would result in **under-specification of performance expectations** and **loss of semantic precision**.



## Formalization of Specialized Competencies

To address this issue, the CSP adopts **explicit competency specialization**, yielding the following refined competencies:

* **C07.01 — Develop Integrated Multi-Agent Solutions Using Turing Machines**
  A specialization of C07, reflecting **system-level construction, coordination, and validation** of multiple Turing Machines.

* **C08.01 — Analyze the Adequacy of Turing Machine Variants in Integrated Systems**
  A specialization of C08, elevating the competency from conceptual recognition to **analytical evaluation of model suitability** under global constraints.

* **C09.01 — Apply and Coordinate Turing Machine Variants in Integrated Systems**
  A specialization of C09, emphasizing **selection, adaptation, and coordination** of variants based on **system-wide requirements**.

These specializations preserve the **core intent and knowledge base** of their parent competencies while introducing **additional contextual constraints and higher cognitive engagement**.




## Methodological Implications within the CSP

This specialization strategy has several methodological advantages:

* **Preservation of Original Competencies**
  C07–C09 remain valid, reusable competencies for foundational and intermediate tasks.

* **Explicit Modeling of Progression**
  The emergence of C07.01–C09.01 makes competency progression **explicit rather than implicit**, avoiding hidden cognitive inflation.

* **Improved Assessability**
  Each specialized competency has clearly defined expectations aligned with the actual task demands.

* **Ontological Soundness**
  Specialization is used strictly to represent **contextual scope expansion**, not conceptual generalization, avoiding improper subsumption or aggregation.

## Synthesis and Methodological Implications

This comparative framework demonstrates that the transition from **C07–C09** to **C07.01–C09.01** is not a matter of redefining competencies, but of **explicitly capturing a controlled expansion in instructional scope and cognitive demand**.

The specializations:

* preserve the **core semantic identity** of the base competencies;
* make **progression explicit and assessable**;
* prevent hidden inflation of expectations;
* align competency modeling with authentic instructional complexity.

As such, the specialization mechanism functions as a **precision instrument within the CSP**, ensuring that competency specifications remain both **reusable and context-sensitive** as problem complexity evolves.


> *Competency specialization in this work does not reflect conceptual generalization, but contextual scope expansion. Specialized competencies preserve the semantic core of their base counterparts while introducing additional constraints that emerge from integrated and system-level problem contexts.*




## Conclusion

The introduction of **C07.01, C08.01, and C09.01** represents a deliberate and methodologically grounded refinement of the competency model. Rather than relying on abstract hierarchies or composite constructs, the CSP explicitly captures **how competencies evolve when applied to increasingly complex instructional contexts**.

This approach ensures that the competency framework remains **precise, extensible, and pedagogically faithful**, while supporting the documentation of authentic learning progression across tasks.
