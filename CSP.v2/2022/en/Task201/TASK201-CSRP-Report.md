# CSRP-Report  
## Competency Specification Review & Refinement  
### Task201 – Surveillance Drone Prototype


## 1. Introduction

This **Competency Specification Review & Refinement Report (CSRP-Report)** summarizes the results of an **expert-based review** of the competencies reused in **Task201 – Surveillance Drone Prototype**, conducted as part of **Phase 2 (Expert Review)** of the **Competency Specification Process (CSP)**.

The review aimed to examine the **alignment, scope adequacy, and activation patterns** of the reused competencies when applied to a realistic, constraint-driven instructional task. Rather than proposing new competencies, the focus of the review was on identifying **tensions, refinements, and clarification needs** that emerged from expert analysis of the CSP-Report.



## 2. Overview of Reviewed Competencies

Based on the CSP-Report for Task201, the following competencies were reviewed by domain experts:

- C02 – Justify the use of Deterministic Finite Automata (DFAs)  
- C03 – Test Automata Using Simulators  
- C04 – Define Regular Expressions for Finite Automata  
- C05 – Write a Technical Report  
- C06 – Develop problem solutions using Finite State Machines  
- C11 – Identify Patterns in Finite State Machines  

Overall, the review identified a **strong correspondence** between the selected competencies, the task objectives, and the expected learner artifacts, particularly with respect to formal modeling and validation activities.



## 3. Competency-Level Review Findings

### 3.1 C06 – Develop problem solutions using Finite State Machines

**Review Outcome:** Strong alignment  
**Detected Tension:** None significant  

The expert review identified **C06** as the central competency supporting Task201. Experts agreed that the competency’s scope adequately covers the modeling of a reactive surveillance system using FSMs, including event detection, state transitions, and transmission logic.

No scope mismatch or overgeneralization was detected. The review confirmed that C06 is sufficiently generic to support **context-rich, real-world scenarios** while preserving formal rigor.



### 3.2 C02 – Justify the use of Deterministic Finite Automata (DFAs)

**Review Outcome:** Strong alignment  
**Detected Tension:** Low  

The review indicated that **C02** was activated primarily at a **reasoning and justification level**. Experts noted that, although explicit comparisons between DFA and NFA were not required as deliverables, learners were expected to justify determinism and abstraction choices inherent in the proposed FSM models.

The review confirmed that C02 appropriately supports such analytical justification, even when not manifested as a standalone artifact.



### 3.3 C03 – Test Automata Using Simulators

**Review Outcome:** Very strong alignment  
**Detected Tension:** None  

Expert feedback consistently highlighted **C03** as one of the most clearly operationalized competencies in Task201. Simulation using **JFLAP** was an explicit requirement and directly observable in student artifacts.

No refinements were recommended for this competency in the context of this task.



### 3.4 C04 – Define Regular Expressions for Finite Automata

**Review Outcome:** Partial alignment  
**Detected Tension:** Moderate  

The expert review detected a **scope tension** in the reuse of **C04**. While the task did not explicitly require learners to construct regular expressions, experts observed that students were expected to engage in **symbolic and structural reasoning** about event patterns and FSM complexity, particularly when deriving estimation rules for states and transitions.

Reviewers emphasized that, in Task201, C04 was activated at an **analytical and conceptual level**, rather than through the production of regular-expression artifacts. This led to the recommendation that the competency’s scope be interpreted more flexibly during reuse, or that its activation mode be explicitly documented in the CSP.



### 3.5 C11 – Identify Patterns in Finite State Machines

**Review Outcome:** Strong alignment  
**Detected Tension:** Low  

The review identified **C11** as well aligned with the abstraction and generalization demands of the task. Experts noted that recognizing structural regularities in FSM design was essential for state minimization and for deriving general estimation rules.

C11 was viewed as a complementary competency that reinforces higher-level reasoning beyond concrete model construction.



### 3.6 C05 – Write a Technical Report

**Review Outcome:** Strong alignment  
**Detected Tension:** None  

Experts confirmed that **C05** aligned directly with the requirement to produce a structured technical report in **SBC format**. The competency was activated consistently across documentation, justification, and communication activities.

No task-specific refinements were identified.



## 4. Cross-Competency Observations

The expert review highlighted that the reused competencies were activated in **distinct modes**, including:

- **Artifact-driven activation** (C03, C05),
- **Model-construction activation** (C06),
- **Reasoning-oriented activation** (C02, C11),
- **Analytical/symbolic activation** (C04).

Experts emphasized that such variation does not indicate misalignment, but rather reflects the **context-sensitive nature of competency reuse**, reinforcing the need for explicit documentation of activation modes in CSP artifacts.



## 5. Implications for CSP Refinement

Based on the expert review findings, a set of **procedural refinements** to the **Competency Specification Process (CSP)** was identified. These refinements do not alter the core structure of the CSP; rather, they clarify and strengthen how existing phases and artifacts are operationalized during iterative application.

Specifically, the review indicated the need to:

- **Explicitly record the mode of competency activation** when competencies are reused across different task contexts (e.g., constructive, analytical, justificatory, or artifact-driven), in order to improve transparency and interpretability of competency reuse.
- **Systematically document scope tensions** observed in broadly defined competencies—particularly those involving symbolic or analytical reasoning—as part of the CSP review cycle, supporting informed refinement without unnecessary proliferation of new competencies.

Together, these adjustments reinforce the CSP as an **iterative, evidence-informed process**, in which competency definitions and reuse practices are progressively clarified through expert review and authentic task application.



## 6. Conclusion

The expert-based CSRP-Report for Task201 indicates that the CSP effectively supports not only the specification but also the **critical evaluation and iterative refinement of competencies**. The tensions identified—most notably in the reuse of C04—were interpreted by reviewers as opportunities for increased semantic clarity rather than as deficiencies in the competency framework.

Overall, the review confirms Task201 as a robust case illustrating how expert feedback contributes to the **progressive maturation of competency models** within the CSP.
