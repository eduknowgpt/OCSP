# Task25.22 — School Census and Computational Thinking in Natural Language

## Task Description — School Census and Computational Thinking in Natural Language

In this task, you will work with a **school census dataset** and progressively learn how to **describe algorithms using natural language** to process and analyze structured information.

The activity is organized into **four steps of increasing complexity**. In each step, you will describe, in words, **how a computer could process student records** to count, classify, and analyze data. You will not write code. Instead, you will focus on **clear, logical, and well-structured explanations** of the steps involved.

At each new stage, you will **reuse and adapt your previous solution**, adding new data fields and conditions. This will help you understand how algorithms can be **extended, generalized, and improved**, which is a key idea in **Computational Thinking**.

By the end of the task, you should be able to clearly explain how data is processed step by step, anticipate results, and reason about the correctness and efficiency of your solution.



## What You Are Expected to Learn

By completing all four steps, you should be able to:

1. Understand what a **data record** is and identify the fields needed to solve a problem (for example: Name, Sex, Myopia).
2. Describe an **algorithm in natural language**, organizing the steps in a clear and logical order.
3. Use **conditions and logical decisions** (such as “if”, “else”, “and”, “or”) to classify and count information.
4. Calculate **totals and averages** by describing how values are accumulated and processed.
5. Adapt and extend an existing algorithm to handle **new fields and combined conditions**.
6. Review your own solution, identifying **logical errors, unnecessary repetitions, or opportunities to simplify and improve** it.
7. Relate what you are doing to **real-world situations** where data is collected and analyzed, such as school censuses, surveys, or health statistics.



## Task Stages (Progressive Steps)

### Step 1 — Census by Sex

**Your task:**  
Describe, in natural language, an algorithm that reads a list of student records containing the fields `Name` and `Sex` and produces:

- the total number of boys,
- the total number of girls,
- the total number of students.

Your description should explain **how each record is processed** and **how the counts are updated**.

**Key ideas:**  
Sequential processing, simple conditions, counting.



### Step 2 — Census by Sex and Myopia

**Your task:**  
Extend your algorithm from Step 1 by including a new field called `Myopia`.

Describe how the algorithm should:

- count students with myopia by sex,
- compute the **average number of students with myopia** for each sex,
- compute the **overall average**.

Reuse the structure of your previous solution and clearly explain what changes.

**Key ideas:**  
Accumulators, averages, numeric comparison.



### Step 3 — Census with Two Conditions (Myopia and Hypermetropia)

**Your task:**  
Adapt your algorithm to handle two visual conditions: `Myopia` and `Hypermetropia`.

Describe how the algorithm should:

- count students by sex and by condition,
- compute specific averages for each group,
- handle cases where a student may have one condition or the other.

Explain how **logical operators** (such as AND / OR) are used to combine conditions.

**Key ideas:**  
Composite conditions, logical operators, generalization of previous logic.



### Step 4 — Census with Three Conditions and Combinations

**Your task:**  
Describe a complete algorithm that processes three conditions: `Myopia`, `Hypermetropia`, and `Astigmatism`.

Your algorithm should:

- compute totals and averages,
- identify students with **combined conditions** (for example, Myopia and Astigmatism),
- organize the solution in a clear and reusable way.

At this stage, your description should show a **general and well-structured solution**, not just isolated calculations.

**Key ideas:**  
Combination of conditions, repetition, modular reasoning, solution refinement.



## Final Notes

- Write your algorithms as if you were **explaining to another person or to a computer what to do, step by step**.
- Be precise, but use **clear natural language**, not programming code.
- Each step builds on the previous one — **reuse your ideas and improve them**.
- Focus on **clarity, logic, and correctness** rather than speed.

This task will help you think like a computer scientist by organizing information, making decisions based on data, and explaining processes clearly.
