# Task25.4: School Census and Computational Thinking in Natural Language

## Task Description and Learning Objectives – “School Census and Computational Thinking in Natural Language”

The task, structured in **four progressive steps**, has as its central purpose the **incremental development of the ability to describe algorithms in natural language** to process and analyze **structured records**.  
This ability is one of the pillars of **Computational Thinking**, particularly in the dimensions of **abstraction, decomposition, and problem generalization**, and it also supports the development of competencies in **data representation and logical reasoning**.

Each stage reuses and extends the logical structures of the previous step—promoting **conceptual, syntactic, and cognitive reuse**—and gradually introduces **new fields, conditions, and relationships between variables**, leading learners to higher levels of abstraction and autonomy in formulating algorithmic solutions.

### Pedagogical Justification

The activity was designed to integrate logical-mathematical reasoning with computational thinking, bringing students closer to a programmer’s way of thinking without requiring prior knowledge of a formal language.  
By working in natural language, the focus is on **organizing thought, clarifying instructions, and anticipating results**, preparing learners for future transitions to pseudocode and programming.  
The progression also encourages reflection on **algorithm quality and efficiency**, which are fundamental elements in Computing education.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of the four-step sequence, the learner is expected to be able to:

1. **Understand the structure of a data record** and recognize the fields relevant to a given problem (e.g., Name, Sex, Myopia).

2. **Describe algorithms in natural language in a logical and ordered way**, detailing inputs, conditions, and outputs.

3. **Use logical operators and conditional structures** to classify, filter, and count information based on record attributes.

4. **Compute totals and averages**, using algorithmic reasoning and accumulator variables, demonstrating an understanding of data-processing cycles.

5. **Refine and generalize solutions**, adapting previous algorithms to handle new fields (Myopia, Hypermetropia, Astigmatism) and combining multiple conditions.

6. **Evaluate the coherence and efficiency of an algorithmic solution**, identifying redundancies, logical errors, and opportunities for modularization or reuse.

7. **Relate algorithms to real-world data collection and analysis contexts**, understanding the social and ethical relevance of automated information processing (e.g., school censuses, diagnostics, surveys).


## Task Stages (with Increasing Complexity)

### Step 1 — Census by Sex

**Description:**  
> The learner describes, in natural language, an algorithm that reads records containing `Name` and `Sex` and outputs the total number of boys, girls, and students.

> **Key concepts:** Simple data structure, conditional counting, sequential processing.  

> **Cognitive complexity:** *Low (Apply)*.


### Step 2 — Census by Sex and Myopia

**Description:**  
> Building on the structure of Step 1, the learner extends the algorithm to include the `Myopia` field, computing specific counts and averages by sex and an overall average.

> **Key concepts:** Introduction to accumulator variables, computation of averages, comparison of numeric values.  

> **Cognitive complexity:** *Medium (Analyze and Apply)*.  

> **Reuse:** Reuses the reading and counting structure from Step 1.


### Step 3 — Census with Two Conditions (Myopia and Hypermetropia)

**Description:**  
> The algorithm now considers two visual impairment attributes (`Myopia` and `Hypermetropia`), performing counts and specific averages by sex and type.

> **Key concepts:** Composite conditional structures, use of logical operators (AND/OR), generalization of calculation functions.  

> **Cognitive complexity:** *Medium–High (Analyze)*.  

> **Reuse:** Expands the model from Step 2 with new variables and composite logical conditions.


### Step 4 — Census with Three Conditions and Combinations (Myopia, Hypermetropia, Astigmatism)

**Description:**  
> The learner describes the complete algorithm, considering three attributes and their intersections. They must calculate totals, averages, and combinations (e.g., students with Myopia and Astigmatism).

> **Key concepts:** Repetition structures, combinatorial logic, functional modularization.  

> **Cognitive complexity:** *High (Create and Evaluate)*.  

> **Reuse:** Builds on the structure of Step 3, adding the handling of combinations and conceptual modularization of the algorithm.
