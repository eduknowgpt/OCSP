# Competency Specification Report: Cryptarithms – A Playful Approach to Teaching Basic Cryptography Concepts in K–12 Education

## **Introduction**

Building on the foundational CSP methodology, this report presents the application of the **Competency Authoring phase** to the instructional activity *“Cryptarithms: A Playful Approach to Teaching Basic Cryptography Concepts in K–12 Education.”*

This learning task integrates **computational thinking**, **logical reasoning**, and **introductory cryptographic concepts** through a ludic and mathematically grounded approach. Designed for students in **upper elementary and high school**, the activity leverages cryptarithmic puzzles as concrete, manipulable artifacts that allow learners to explore **symbolic representation**, **substitution systems**, **pattern recognition**, and **algorithmic reasoning** in an accessible and engaging format.

In alignment with the **BNCC Computing competencies** and contemporary approaches to K–12 computing education, the task encourages students to **solve and create cryptarithmic challenges**, thereby promoting autonomy, creativity, persistence, and the development of problem-solving strategies rooted in abstraction and decomposition.
Within the CSP framework, cryptarithms serve as the **Instructional Entity** from which we derive knowledge requirements, cognitive processes, dispositions, and competency specifications essential to successful task performance.

The following sections present the **Instructional Entity Analysis**, enumerate the **knowledge elements**, identify the **learning objectives**, and articulate the **competency specifications** aligned with the OntoKSD model.


## 1. Instructional Entity Analysis

### Title

* *Cryptarithms: A Playful Approach to Teaching Basic Cryptography Concepts in K–12 Education*


### Description

* Learners face the problem of **solving and creating cryptarithmic puzzles as a means to explore foundational concepts of cryptography**, such as symbolic substitution, information encoding, pattern recognition, and logical consistency. Through the manipulation of digits, symbols, and arithmetic operations, learners develop strategies to decode and construct valid mathematical expressions, strengthening their computational thinking and understanding of how cryptographic systems rely on structured symbol manipulation.



### Solution Development Process

Describe the expected learner approach:

* **Analyze** the structure and constraints of a given cryptarithmic puzzle (distinct digits, carry operations, positional alignment, validity of arithmetic outcomes).

* **Decompose** the problem into smaller logical steps, identifying patterns and constraints that reduce the search space.

* **Explore** hypotheses by testing possible digit assignments and verifying consistency with the puzzle’s rules.

* **Validate** results through systematic checking of operations and symbol–number alignment.

* **Create** an original cryptarithmic challenge by designing a mathematically consistent puzzle, choosing symbols, defining valid solutions, and documenting the reasoning behind the construction.

* **Communicate** solutions and strategies clearly—both orally and in written form—justifying logical decisions and demonstrating understanding of substitution-based cryptographic reasoning.



### Expected Outcomes

Learners should produce:

* Correctly solved cryptarithmic puzzles demonstrating logical consistency and valid symbol–digit mappings.

* A self-created cryptarithmic challenge that adheres to arithmetical and structural constraints.

* Written or verbal explanations detailing the reasoning process, problem decomposition, and validation strategies used.

* Evidence of collaborative reasoning (in group tasks), including peer discussion, shared hypotheses, and refinement of solutions.

* Demonstrations of cryptographic awareness (symbol substitution, encoding, reversible transformations).

* Artifacts (worksheets, solution steps, created puzzles) that document the learner’s analytical and creative process.


### Acquisition Context

This learning task is developed within **K–12 computing and mathematics instruction**, in alignment with the **BNCC Computing competencies** and the **Computing in Basic Education Supplement (SBC, 2023)**.
It is suitable for **classroom environments, computational thinking workshops, project-based learning sessions**, or enrichment activities that integrate mathematics and introductory cryptography.
The context emphasizes **hands-on manipulation**, collaborative exploration, and creative puzzle design, allowing students to engage deeply with abstraction, symbolic reasoning, and logical inference in a playful and motivating setting.



### Target Audience Profile
* **Educational Level:** Upper Elementary and High School students (Grades 6–12).

* **Prior Knowledge:** Basic arithmetic operations, familiarity with number representation, initial exposure to mathematical problem solving.

* **Programming/Crypto Experience:** No formal knowledge required; introductory understanding of patterns, logic, or encoding is sufficient.

* **Learning Needs:** Concrete examples, guided reasoning, visually structured problems, opportunities for both individual and collaborative exploration.

* **Expected Dispositions:** Curiosity, persistence, creativity, collaborative engagement, attention to detail, and willingness to revise strategies based on feedback.



### Proficiency Scale

Scores range from **0.0 to 10.0**, in increments of **0.1**, based on:

* **Accuracy** of solving cryptarithmic puzzles and validity of created challenges.
* **Logical consistency** of the reasoning and explanation.
* **Creativity** in the construction of new puzzles.
* **Use of computational thinking skills** (decomposition, pattern recognition, abstraction).
* **Clarity** of oral or written communication.
* **Collaboration and engagement** during the activity.
* **Ethical behavior**, such as academic honesty and respect for peers.

## 3. Learning Objectives Identification

By the end of the task, the learner should be able to:

1. **LO1 – Analyze** the structure and constraints of cryptarithmic puzzles to identify valid symbol–digit relationships.

2. **LO2 – Apply** decomposition and pattern recognition strategies to reduce the solution search space.

3. **LO3 – Solve** cryptarithmic problems using logical inference and arithmetic consistency.

4. **LO4 – Validate** solutions through systematic checking, ensuring compliance with puzzle constraints.

5. **LO5 – Create** original cryptarithmic challenges that demonstrate mathematical correctness and symbolic coherence.

6. **LO6 – Explain** the reasoning used to solve or create puzzles, articulating steps clearly in oral or written form.

7. **LO7 – Collaborate** with peers to compare strategies, negotiate interpretations, and refine solutions.

8. **LO8 – Reflect** on the cryptographic principles involved (substitution, encoding, pattern stabilization) and relate them to practical contexts.

### CT25.3.1 – Solve Cryptarithmic Challenges by Applying Logical Reasoning and Problem Decomposition

### Competency Title

    Solve cryptarithmic challenges by applying logical reasoning and problem decomposition.


### Textual Description

This competency involves the ability to solve cryptarithmic challenges in which letters or symbols represent unknown digits in mathematically valid operations.
The learner analyzes the constraints imposed by the operation (e.g., addition, multiplication), decomposes the puzzle into smaller parts (columns, carry operations, repetition patterns), and formulates hypotheses for the possible values of each symbol.

By testing, rejecting, and refining these hypotheses, the learner exercises logical reasoning, systematic problem search, and persistence when facing complexity. The activity is preferably performed in groups, encouraging strategy discussion, comparison of reasoning approaches, and collaborative refinement of solutions.


### Knowledge Specification

- **Basic Arithmetic Operations**

  - The learner applies knowledge of arithmetic operations (addition, multiplication, carry procedures) to interpret and solve challenges in which numbers are substituted by letters or symbols. They must ensure that the entire operation remains valid for all digits, respecting the rules of the decimal number system.

  * **Bloom’s Alignment:** Apply
  * **Knowledge–Skill Pairing:** Basic Arithmetic / Apply
  * **Verb Annotation:** Use, Calculate, Verify, Implement


- **Analytical Thinking**

  - The learner analyzes the structure of the puzzle, identifies dependencies between columns (e.g., the effect of the carry), and makes inferences based on available clues. Analytical thinking enables them to organize the search space more efficiently, quickly discarding impossible combinations.

  * **Bloom’s Alignment:** Analyze
  * **Knowledge–Skill Pairing:** Analytical Thinking / Analyze
  * **Verb Annotation:** Distinguish, Infer, Deduce, Examine

- **Problem Decomposition**

  - The learner decomposes the complete challenge into smaller parts: first determining more evident digits, then adjusting intermediate values and revising the global solution. This decomposition makes the complex task more manageable and reinforces the idea that large problems can be solved step by step.

  * **Bloom’s Alignment:** Apply
  * **Knowledge–Skill Pairing:** Problem Decomposition / Apply
  * **Verb Annotation:** Break down, Organize, Structure, Sequence


### Disposition Specification

* **Persistent** — maintains effort in the search for a solution, even when earlier attempts fail, revising hypotheses and checking calculations without giving up prematurely.

* **Meticulous** — pays careful attention to detail when checking each operation, avoiding distraction-based errors and ensuring consistency across all columns.

* **Collaborative** — actively participates in group discussions, listening to peers’ strategies, justifying reasoning, and reaching consensus-based solutions.



### BNCC-Aligned Competencies

### General Computing Competencies (High School)

* **Competency 1** — Solve problems by proposing and analyzing computational solutions: treating a cryptarithmic challenge as a structured problem reinforces systematic problem-solving strategies.

* **Competency 4** — Build knowledge using computational techniques in creative and ethical ways: creating and analyzing strategies for the puzzle strengthens logical and computational reasoning.


### Specific Competencies (Elementary and High School)

* **EF04CO03** — Create and simulate algorithms using simple and nested sequences and repetitions (orally, in writing, or pictographically): solving a cryptarithm resembles describing an algorithm that tests digit combinations and verifies operations.

* **EF15CO05** — Encode information in different ways for storage, manipulation, and transmission: using letters to represent digits concretely illustrates symbolic information encoding.

* **EF09CO05** — Analyze cryptographic techniques for storing and transmitting data: the activity introduces symbolic substitution as a simplified form of encryption.

* **EM13CO01** — Explore and construct solutions to problems by reusing parts of existing solutions: recognizing patterns across cryptarithms leads students to reuse reasoning structures and strategies.

### CT25.3.2 – Encode and Decode Information Using Symbols, Relating Cryptarithms to Basic Cryptography Concepts

###  Competency Title 

Encode and decode information using symbols, relating cryptarithms to basic concepts of cryptography.



###  Textual Description 

This competency focuses on understanding that numbers, letters, and other symbols can represent information in encoded form.
In the context of cryptarithms, the learner observes that each letter corresponds to a digit, and that the “hidden message” is precisely the numerical assignment that makes the operation valid.

This idea connects naturally to basic substitution-based cryptography, where letters or blocks of text are mapped to other symbols.
Through this analogy, learners begin to understand that the security of a message depends on the encoding rule and the secrecy behind it, approaching the foundational principles of digital security.



###  Knowledge Specification (KS) 

- **Information Encoding**

  - The learner understands that, in order to manipulate and transmit data, it must be encoded into symbolic representations that can be processed by people or machines (e.g., letters in place of digits). This comprehension forms the conceptual bridge between cryptarithms, digital encoding, and cryptography.

  * **Bloom’s Alignment:** Understand
  * **Knowledge–Skill Pairing:** Information Encoding / Understand
  * **Verb Annotation:** Explain, Interpret, Describe, Recognize, Clarify



- **Fundamentals of Cryptography**

  - The learner understands, at an introductory level, the role of cryptography as a technique for making messages unreadable to those without the key, and identifies cryptarithms as a playful analogue of substitution-based problems.

  * **Bloom’s Alignment:** Understand
  * **Knowledge–Skill Pairing:** Cryptography Fundamentals / Understand
  * **Verb Annotation:** Explain, Summarize, Identify, Distinguish, Comprehend



- **Symbolic Representation**

  - The learner applies symbolic representation by consistently associating letters with digits and verifying whether the proposed mapping preserves the correctness of the operation. They consciously manipulate these symbolic forms to test different mappings and evaluate their validity.

  * **Bloom’s Alignment:** Apply
  * **Knowledge–Skill Pairing:** Symbolic Representation / Apply
  * **Verb Annotation:** Use, Manipulate, Test, Validate



## **Disposition Specification**

* **Curious** — demonstrates interest in uncovering “what is behind the code,” questioning how information can be concealed and revealed.

* **Critical** — reflects on the strength or weakness of simple encoding rules, connecting the puzzle to real-world password and data security.

* **Responsible** — understands and discusses that cryptography and encoding must be used ethically and safely, respecting privacy and rights.



### BNCC-Aligned Competencies

### General Computing Competencies (High School)

* **Competency 2** — Critically analyze computational artifacts, identifying vulnerabilities and ensuring information security and confidentiality: discussions on cryptography and encoding connect the puzzle directly to cybersecurity.

* **Competency 4** — Build knowledge using computational techniques, producing artifacts (including challenges) related to encoding and cryptography.



### **Specific Competencies**

* **EF04CO04 & EF04CO05** — Understand and practice data encoding for digital representation: the systematic association of letters with digits is an exercise in symbolic information encoding.

* **EF15CO05** — Encode information in different ways for storage, manipulation, and transmission: learners experience the idea that the same value can be represented with different symbols.

* **EF09CO05** — Analyze cryptographic techniques for storing and transmitting data: cryptarithms serve as a conceptual entry point to discuss cryptography and its relevance in the digital world.

### CT25.3.3 – Formulate and Test Algorithmic Strategies to Systematically Solve Cryptarithmic Challenges

### Competency Title

    Formulate and test algorithmic strategies to systematically solve cryptarithmic challenges.


### Textual Description

This competency concerns the construction of step-by-step strategies (algorithms) to solve cryptarithms in an organized manner, rather than through random trial-and-error.
The learner articulates, in natural language, schemes such as: *“choose a possible digit for the first letter, compute the carry, verify consistency in the next columns, backtrack if a contradiction arises.”*

By making these steps explicit, the learner approaches algorithmic thinking, realizing that they can generalize their strategy to other puzzles, including computational contexts (pseudocode, future implementations).
This articulates the foundations of designing systematic, rule-based procedures for symbolic problem solving.


### Knowledge Specification

- **Algorithmic Thinking**

  - The learner applies algorithmic thinking by organizing the solution into well-defined steps, with clear decision criteria (if–then–else; repeat while possibilities remain). Even without programming, they structure the solution as a conceptual algorithm.

  * **Bloom’s Alignment:** Apply
  * **Knowledge–Skill Pairing:** Algorithmic Thinking / Apply
  * **Verb Annotation:** Organize, Implement, Execute, Sequence



- **Algorithm Design (Natural or Pictographic Language)**

  - The learner creates algorithm descriptions—oral, written, or pictographic—to explain how to solve a cryptarithm: which digits to test first, when to backtrack, and how to check consistency. This strengthens the ability to express processes precisely and systematically.

  * **Bloom’s Alignment:** Create
  * **Knowledge–Skill Pairing:** Algorithm Design / Create
  * **Verb Annotation:** Construct, Design, Compose, Formulate



- **Logical Operators**

  - The learner applies logical connectives (and, or, not) and relational operators (equal, not equal) when reasoning about digit constraints: *“if A = 2 and E = 8, then C cannot be 2 or 8.”*
  This reinforces the logical foundations necessary for modeling conditions in algorithms.

  * **Bloom’s Alignment:** Apply
  * **Knowledge–Skill Pairing:** Logical Operators / Apply
  * **Verb Annotation:** Evaluate, Compare, Test, Combine



### Disposition Specification

* **Systematic** — organizes reasoning into clear steps, avoiding random guesses and documenting the path taken.

* **Reflective** — revisits strategies, identifies weaknesses, and proposes improvements to make them more efficient.

* **Collaborative** — constructs algorithms together with peers, comparing strategies and consolidating a consensus-based approach.



### BNCC-Aligned Competencies

### General Competencies (High School)

* **Competency 1** — Solve problems by proposing viable and efficient computational strategies: articulating algorithmic steps concretely fulfills this competency.

* **Competency 5** — Develop solutions collaboratively, integrating computational concepts and procedures: jointly constructing algorithmic strategies directly aligns with this competency.


### Specific Competencies

* **EF04CO03** — Create and simulate algorithms with simple and nested repetitions: repeated digit testing and column traversal mirror conceptual loops.

* **EM13CO02** — Refine solutions across levels of abstraction, from specification to implementation: moving from informal reasoning to structured algorithmic description exemplifies refinement.

### CT25.3.4 – Create, Validate, and Communicate Original Cryptarithmic Challenges, Connecting Them to Information Security Contexts

### Competency Title

    Create, validate, and communicate original cryptarithmic challenges, connecting them to information security contexts.


### Textual Description

  - This competency involves the learner transitioning from being merely a “solver” to becoming an “author” of puzzles. The learner must design consistent cryptarithmic challenges (i.e., puzzles with valid operations and a unique solution), test them, and adjust as needed.

-  Additionally, they must clearly explain the puzzle statement, describe the solution reasoning, and relate the challenge to real-world situations involving encoding and cryptography (e.g., passwords, secret codes, secure messages).

- Creating and exchanging challenges in the classroom reinforces authorship, communication, and a culture of responsible knowledge sharing.



### Knowledge Specification

- **Cryptarithmic**

  - The learner creates new cryptarithmic problems by defining the operation, choosing symbols, and ensuring the solution is coherent. This requires reverse planning: starting from a target outcome and deriving a puzzle that leads to that result.

  * **Bloom’s Alignment:** Create
  * **Knowledge–Skill Pairing:** Cryptarithmic / Create
  * **Verb Annotation:** Design, Construct, Compose, Generate


- **Mathematical Reasoning**

  - The learner analyzes whether the created challenge is well-defined (no ambiguities or multiple solutions), identifying inconsistencies and adjusting the structure. This supports the validation of the puzzle before sharing it.

  * **Bloom’s Alignment:** Analyze
  * **Knowledge–Skill Pairing:** Mathematical Reasoning / Analyze
  * **Verb Annotation:** Analyze, Evaluate, Inspect, Verify, Diagnose


- **Oral Communication**

  - The learner applies oral communication skills to present their challenge and solution, organizing discourse clearly, using proper mathematical and computational vocabulary, and responding to peers’ questions.

  * **Bloom’s Alignment:** Apply
  * **Knowledge–Skill Pairing:** Oral Communication / Apply
  * **Verb Annotation:** Present, Explain, Articulate, Communicate, Respond


### Disposition Specification

* **Creative** — explores different puzzle formats, playful narratives (stories, “magic kingdoms,” thematic riddles), and engaging ways to challenge peers.

* **Ethical** — values authorship, acknowledges inspirations, avoids plagiarism, and respects peers’ work.

* **Communicative** — expresses ideas clearly when writing and presenting the puzzle, facilitating group understanding.



### BNCC-Aligned Competencies

### General Competencies (High School)

* **Competency 4** — Produce computational artifacts creatively, respecting ethical and legal issues: puzzle creation is an educational and playful artifact within this scope.

* **Competency 6** — Express and share information, ideas, and computational solutions in various languages: explaining the puzzle and solution directly fulfills this.

* **Competency 7** — Act with respect, responsibility, and autonomy, drawing on Computing knowledge: discussing ethics, authorship, and responsible use of simple cryptography.



### Specific Competencies

* **EF09CO05** — Analyze cryptographic techniques for data protection: learners relate puzzles to real-world situations where cryptography protects information.

* **EF09CO09** — Create or use digital content while understanding ethical issues of authorship: relevant when puzzles are typed, shared, or integrated into digital artifacts.

* **EM13CO01** — Reuse parts of existing solutions: learners may draw inspiration from solved puzzles and design variations with modified operations, numbers, or symbols.
