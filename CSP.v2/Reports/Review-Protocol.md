# CSP Review Protocol


## Introduction

This **Competency Specification Review Protocol** serves as a formal instrument for **Phase 2 – Expert Review** within the **Competency Specification Process (CSP)**. Its primary objective is to guide structured, rigorous evaluation of competency specifications drafted in Phase 1, ensuring they meet established standards of:

* **Accuracy**: Validates that competency statements faithfully reflect domain knowledge and theoretical foundations.
* **Clarity**: Checks that each competency is articulated clearly, unambiguously, and with measurable precision.
* **Alignment**: Confirms that competencies are logically and meaningfully linked to learning objectives, domain knowledge, and required skills.

The protocol is intentionally designed to balance prescriptive guidance with expert judgment. While it establishes a common framework for review, it also acknowledges that qualified reviewers may suggest complementary improvements that extend beyond the predefined criteria. This flexibility ensures that the review process not only verifies minimum quality thresholds, but also promotes iterative refinement and pedagogical rigor.

The following sections present the evaluation questions, criteria, and validation templates used by experts throughout the review process.


### Key Evaluation Dimensions

* **Knowledge Granularity**
  *Is the level of detail sufficient to capture necessary concepts without unnecessary complexity?*

* **Controlled Vocabulary**
  *Are terminologies and phrasing consistent, professional, and domain-appropriate?*

* **Bloom’s Taxonomy Verbs**
  *Do the selected action verbs align with intended cognitive levels and learning outcomes?*

* **Textual Clarity**
  *Are competency titles and descriptions free from ambiguity and well-constructed for instructional use?*

* **Knowledge Relevance**
  *Is each knowledge component appropriately mapped and essential to the task and its learning goals?*

### Impact of the Review

Expert feedback gathered through this protocol led to significant refinements, including:

* Reorganizing or refining knowledge categories for improved scope and coherence
* Standardizing terminology to enhance consistency across competencies
* Restructuring competency statements to strengthen instructional alignment
* Enhancing precision and clarity in wording to support assessment and implementation

This protocol ensures an iterative, transparent, and pedagogically anchored review process that enhances the validity, usability, and educational impact of competency specifications for PBL tasks.




## Scope of Use

* Who: Expert reviewers (subject-matter experts, competency designers, instructional designers)

* When: Implemented during Phase 2, immediately following initial competency drafting (Phase 1)

* Why: To confirm that competencies are accurate, clear, aligned, and contextually grounded, and to produce validated specifications ready for semantic structuring in Phase 3.


## Scoring Rubric & Decision Thresholds
These criteria are used across review stages to ensure consistency:

**Score Definitions**
- Yes – Fully meets criterion with clear supporting evidence

- Maybe – Partially meets criterion or requires clarification

- No – Does not meet criterion and requires revision

**Decision Thresholds**
- Approved → All items rated Yes, or at most one Maybe

- Needs Revision → Any item rated No, or multiple Maybes



## Instructional Entity Description Analysis

**Objective:** Verify that the instructional entity foundation sufficiently supports the competency definitions.

**Objective:**  
Ensure that the instructional entity provides a sound and coherent foundation for competency specification. This includes verifying whether the learning task is clearly articulated, pedagogically relevant, and adequately detailed to support the identification of required knowledge, skills, and dispositions.

The **Instructional Entity Evaluation* table serves as a structured instrument for assessing the clarity, completeness, and alignment of key task elements. It helps reviewers determine whether the task description can validly support the construction and validation of well-grounded competencies.


| **Aspect**               | **Rating Options**              | **Evaluator Notes**                                                      |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Task Title**           | Clear / Needs Revision      | Does the title accurately reflect the task’s focus and scope?            |
| **Problem Description**  | Clear / Needs Clarification | Is the problem statement explicit, complete, and contextually relevant?  |
| **Solution Development** |  Appropriate /  Incomplete    | Are solution steps actionable and aligned with defined competencies?     |
| **Expected Outcomes**    |  Well Specified /  Misaligned | Do outcomes clearly relate to the tasks and competencies being assessed? |

> **Tip:** Note how each task element supports or limits the competencies (e.g., vague descriptions may hide essential required knowledge/skills).

**Decision Thresholds**
- Approved → All items rated Clear, Appropriate, Well Specified.

- Needs Revision → Any item rated Needs Revision/Clarification, Incomplete, Misaligned


## Review of Enumerated Knowledge

**Objective:** Ensure that the knowledge components identified in the competency specification are conceptually valid, sufficiently comprehensive, and appropriately scoped to support the instructional task. This involves verifying that each knowledge element is directly aligned with the task’s cognitive demands and grounded in established domain frameworks.

To guide this analysis, the following table provides a structured instrument for evaluating the relevance, granularity, and adequacy of the listed knowledge components. Reviewers should assess whether the selected knowledge areas are essential to task completion, detailed enough to enable competency acquisition, and semantically coherent with the intended learning outcomes.

| **Criterion**         | **Yes / Maybe / No** | **Evaluator Notes**                                                        |
| --------------------- | -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Comprehensiveness** |                      | Are all essential knowledge areas present?                                  |
| **Relevance**         |                      | Does each knowledge element directly support task or competency goals?     |
| **Appropriateness**   |                      | Is the level of detail correctly balanced—not too general or too granular? |

> **Tip:** Cross-check knowledge items against authoritative classification schemes (e.g., ACM Computing Classification System, CC2020 Body of Knowledge) to ensure terminological and conceptual consistency.



## Review of Learning Objectives (LOs)

**Objective:** Assess whether the learning objectives (LOs) are clearly stated, educationally relevant, and properly aligned with the instructional goals of the task. The next table provides a structured framework for evaluating the completeness and relevance of each LO, ensuring that they encompass both explicit and implicit learning expectations. 

| **Criterion**       | **Yes / Maybe / No** | **Evaluator Notes**                                                         |
| ------------------- | -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Completeness**    |                      | Do the LOs cover both explicit and implicit learning goals?           |
| **Relevance**       |                      | Are all LOs essential within the context of the task?               |

> **Tip:** Use Bloom’s verbs to check alignment with intended cognitive levels.




## Review of Competency Definitions

### Competency Title & Description

**Objective:** Assess the clarity, precision, contextual relevance, and scope of each competency's title and description. This ensures the definition is focused, unambiguous, and well-aligned with the instructional task. See the next table for the evaluation criteria used in this review.

| **Subcomponent**           | **Yes / Maybe / No** | **Evaluator Notes**                                        |
| -------------------------- | -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Title Clarity**          |                      | Is it concise, descriptive, and focused?                   |
| **Description Precision**  |                      | Is the phrasing clear, measurable, and free of ambiguity?  |
| **Contextual Application** |                      | Is the competency tied explicitly to the task scenario?     |
| **Scope Coverage**         |                      | Does it include all necessary elements without redundancy? |



### Knowledge Evaluation

**Objective:** Ensure that each competency’s knowledge components are robust, theoretically grounded, and appropriately scoped for the task context. Evaluation includes theoretical validity, relevance, comprehensiveness, and granularity. Detailed criteria are presented in the next table.

| **Criterion**            | **Yes / Maybe / No** | **Evaluator Notes**                                                             |
| ------------------------ | -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Theoretical Validity** |                      | Does it accurately reflect essential domain theory or professional knowledge?   |
| **Comprehensiveness**    |                      | Are all relevant knowledge areas included for effective competency performance? |
| **Relevance**            |                      | Is each knowledge element essential and appropriately mapped to the competency? |
| **Granularity & Scope**  |                      | Is the content scoped correctly—not too broad nor overly detailed?              |

> **Tip:** Compare knowledge items against recognized taxonomies (e.g., ACM CCS) to verify appropriate depth and coverage.


### Disposition Evaluation

**Objective:**  Confirm that the competency includes dispositions—such as collaboration, responsibility, or persistence—that are explicitly articulated and behaviorally aligned with the competency's real-world application. Refer to the next table for the evaluation criteria. 

| **Criterion**                   | **Yes / Maybe / No** | **Evaluator Notes**                                                                  |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Behavioral Alignment**        |                      | Does the disposition align logically with the competency’s performance requirements? |
| **Explicit Articulation**       |                      | Is the disposition specified clearly, rather than implied?                           |
| **Relevance & Appropriateness** |                      | Is it a meaningful “habit of mind” that enhances competency in real-world contexts?  |

> **Tip:** Dispositions—such as persistence, collaboration, adaptability—serve as the “glue” that integrates knowledge and skills for effective on-the-job performance.



### Knowledge–Skill Pairing & Bloom Alignment

**Objective:** Verify the conceptual coherence between each knowledge–skill pairing and its cognitive complexity level according to Bloom’s Revised Taxonomy. The evaluation considers verb accuracy, Bloom-level alignment, and the rationale for pairing. The assessment framework is outlined in next table.

| **Criterion**               | **Yes / Maybe / No** | **Evaluator Notes**                                                                                                                                                                |
| --------------------------- | -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Bloom-Level Suitability** |                      | Does the described action (skill) align with an appropriate Bloom’s level?  |
| **Verb Effectiveness**      |                      | Are verbs specific and action-oriented (e.g., “construct,” “analyze,” “evaluate”)                   |
| **Pairing Justification**   |                      | Is it clear how the knowledge element supports the skill?                          |

> **Tip:** Conduct a task breakdown (e.g., “insert coin → validate → dispense”) and ensure each step is captured by a competency that pairs relevant knowledge and skill at the correct cognitive level. 



## Summary of Recommendations

**Objective:** Provide a concise roadmap for refining competencies based on expert reviews.

| **Issue Identified**                     | **Suggested Action**                                    |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Incomplete knowledge coverage            | Add theoretical areas (e.g., FSM states transitions)    |
| Verb-level misalignment (Bloom taxonomy) | Replace “understand” with “analyze” or “construct”      |
| Unclear competency title                 | Make it action-oriented and context-specific            |
| Missing dispositions                     | Include behaviors like “collaboration” or “persistence” |

> **Reflection:** Highlight recurring issues—e.g., inconsistent terminology across competencies—and propose broad changes (e.g., create controlled vocabulary glossary).



##  Conclusion & Next Steps

**Objective:** Decide whether to advance the competency set or revisit earlier drafts.

* **Approved for Phase 3**

  * All review items rated **Yes**, or only *one* rated **Maybe**
  * No items rated **No**
  * **Actions:** Prepare competencies for semantic structuring, link to educational frameworks (e.g., Bloom, ACM CCS)

* **Needs Revision (Return to Phase 1)**

  * Any item rated **No**, or more than one rated **Maybe**
  * **Actions:** Update competency set based on recommendations, re-submit for expert review

**Document Iteration:**

* Record decision: Approved or Returned
* Document next steps, responsible person(s), and timelines for revisions
