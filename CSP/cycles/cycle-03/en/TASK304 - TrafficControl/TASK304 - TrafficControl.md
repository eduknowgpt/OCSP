## 1. Introduction

One problem affecting Brazilian highways is the large number of potholes. There are many causes, including alternating periods of rain and sunshine. Another is the pressure exerted by heavy vehicles at night, when they can travel more freely along the highways.

On the Aratu road in Salvador, technicians from the Bahia Department of Transport Infrastructure (DERBA) have identified many potholes, which have caused incidents and, occasionally, serious accidents.

The technicians believe that access to daily reports on the number of vehicles traveling along the road at night could help reduce pressure on the pavement and consequently minimize pothole damage.

The aim is to use these data to develop a pavement-wear prevention plan. The technicians classify vehicles into three categories:

- Light: up to and including 6 tonnes.
- Heavy: more than 6 and less than 10 tonnes.
- Very heavy: 10 tonnes or more.

Sensors already installed at the entrance and exit of the Aratu road identify and categorize vehicles. The technicians would like a solution that uses information from those sensors to determine:

- how many vehicles in each category traveled along the road during the previous night;
- which category had the largest number of vehicles.

Students in the current Theory of Computation class are studying theoretical machines and believe they can design a system prototype with these functions. They have contacted the DERBA technicians to offer solutions to the problem.

The instructor requires the analysis and presentation to address both practical implementation and the theoretical foundations of the solution. The aim is to connect the real-world problem to Theory of Computation, introduce theoretical models to DERBA's technology team, and demonstrate the relevance of studying this field.
